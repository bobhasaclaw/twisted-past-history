About this project

Twisted Past History

History that twists what you thought you knew — A documentary investigation into the origins of civilization and what we lost when we chose to stay.

This is a ten-part documentary project. It began with a question: why did humans, after two hundred thousand years of mobile life without walls, suddenly start building them? Not as a gradual drift, but as a decisive, across-culture, irreversible shift — one that happened independently in multiple locations around the world within a relatively narrow window of time.

The answer the documentary reaches is not simply "agriculture." Agriculture explains the food supply, not the social transformation. Agriculture explains how more people could live in one place, not why they wanted to. The more interesting question is what happened to human social organization — to the structure of power, belief, and belonging — at the moment when people stopped moving and started staying.

That is what this project is about. Not the pyramids, not the ziggurats, not the Roman roads — though they all make appearances. It is about the moment before all of that, and the mechanism by which a species that had been free for two hundred thousand years made itself into something that required walls, rulers, and the concept of property to function.

Each chapter of the series corresponds to one facet of that question: the archaeology of the first structures, the anthropology of the mobile life that preceded them, the neuroscience of the psychological changes they produced, and the political history of what followed. The ten chapters build on each other to form a complete argument.

For Documentary Production

Each chapter page on this site contains the full written content — both the narrative text for voice-over and the research-based background articles. Production cues are included in a format designed for video reference. The content is written to be spoken aloud — short paragraphs, deliberate rhythm, no jargon without explanation.

Chapters 1 through 3 draw from complete scripts used in the original documentary production process. Chapters 4 through 10 were written from research as the script outline was expanded, and contain fully-developed articles with citations suitable for reference during production.

Sources & Research

This project draws from archaeology (Klaus Schmidt's Göbekli Tepe excavation reports, Necmi Karul's Karahan Tepe publications), anthropology (James Woodburn's Hadza research, Marshall Sahlins' "Original Affluent Society"), neuroscience (dopamine and oxytocin research, awe response studies by Keltner and Haidt), evolutionary biology (Belyaev's fox experiment, domestication syndrome research), and political anthropology (in-group/out-group bias research, credible commitment theory from Thomas Schelling and Douglass North).

Full citations are included in the footer of each chapter page.

The Title

The title — Twisted Past History — is not metaphorical. A conspiracy, in its original sense, is a coming together. A convergence. People conspiring are people acting in concert toward a shared end, often without fully understanding what they are doing or why. The first walls were built through such a convergence — not by a single group with a plan, but by human social biology responding to specific conditions, producing outcomes that no individual intended and that every individual came to accept as natural and inevitable.

This is what the documentary argues: that the transition from mobile life to settled civilization was not a discovery or an achievement, but a trade — one that felt like a discovery because the thing received in exchange (belonging, meaning, connection to place) was more compelling than the thing given up (freedom to leave). And that this trade was made collectively, across the whole species, at roughly the same time — because the conditions that made it possible existed everywhere, and the reward that made it desirable was universal.
